# Human oversight and risk controls

Human review and risk controls are part of the LegalGuard description. The specific controls, approval roles and audit records have not been supplied. This page separates the confirmed presence of human oversight from practical controls that should be documented before the project makes stronger claims.

## Human decision points

For contract analysis, a qualified reviewer should check the source clause, context, suggested change and supporting rationale before adopting or sharing a redline. For drafting, the reviewer should confirm that selected clause-library wording is approved for the intended purpose and context. For legal research, the reviewer should inspect the cited source, its date and jurisdiction, and whether it supports the proposition. For regulatory monitoring, the reviewer should confirm applicability and any deadline before assigning an action. These are prudent review expectations; the actual LegalGuard approval gates are **to be documented**.

An AI-generated suggestion should not silently become an executed contract term, legal conclusion or compliance deadline. A portfolio demonstration should show how a reviewer can accept, edit, reject or escalate an item and how unresolved points remain visible. Whether the product supports those actions is **to be documented**.

## Risk and evidence register

| Risk to manage | Control to explain or demonstrate | Evidence still needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unsupported legal statement | Link proposition to source text and show uncertainty | Source records and verification behavior |
| Missed clause or issue | Make review scope and limitations visible; retain human review | Supported document types and review coverage |
| Inappropriate clause-library language | Show source/version and require context-sensitive approval | Library owner, scope and change history |
| Incorrect redline | Compare against original and require reviewer disposition | Redline behavior, approvals and version record |
| Wrong or stale regulatory alert | Cite official item, date and applicability assumptions | Source inventory, update cadence and alert testing |
| Incorrect deadline | Require human confirmation of trigger, calculation and owner | Deadline rules, time zones and correction path |
| Sensitive matter data exposure | Document access, sharing, retention and deletion boundaries | Actual data-handling and security controls |
| Over-reliance on a dashboard label | Explain what the label means and permit challenge or override | Risk methodology and user guidance |

The controls column is a design and documentation checklist, not a statement that a particular control is live.

## Minimum review record to document

For each material output, consider recording the item reviewed, source or clause reference, tool suggestion, reviewer decision, edits or rationale, unresolved questions and relevant versions/dates. State which fields are actually retained, who can access them and for how long. Do not claim an immutable audit log or specific access model unless confirmed.

## Public demo boundaries

Keep the public demo synthetic. Do not upload internal contracts, legal advice, policies, customer/vendor information, credentials, screenshots or operational details without express approval from the information owner. Use public legal material only where the source is identified and checked. Remove real party names and facts; a fictional label alone is insufficient if the underlying matter remains identifiable.

## Details to confirm

- Who reviews each workflow and who can approve a final output.
- Whether approval is mandatory, configurable or outside the product.
- How suggestions, citations, overrides and unresolved issues are recorded.
- User permissions, external processing, retention and deletion controls.
- Escalation routes and handling of low-confidence or conflicting results.
- Any formal review rubric, training, monitoring or incident process.

Until confirmed, describe human oversight as an intended workflow element, not a verified technical control or measured safety outcome.
